SAN FRANCISCO — A new study released Tuesday by researchers at a major university suggests that moderate coffee consumption may be linked to improved cardiovascular health in adults over the age of forty. The study, which followed more than 50,000 participants over a ten-year period, found that those who drank between two and four cups of coffee per day had a fifteen percent lower risk of heart disease compared to non-drinkers.

However, the lead researcher cautioned that the findings should not be interpreted as a recommendation to increase coffee intake. "Correlation does not imply causation," she said in a press briefing. "There may be lifestyle factors common among moderate coffee drinkers that explain the observed benefits."

The study has been published in a peer-reviewed medical journal and is already generating discussion in the cardiology community. Experts agree that more research is needed before any clinical guidelines should change.
